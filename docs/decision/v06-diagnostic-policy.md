# Decision v0.6 — development retention policy amendment

Authorized by the user on 2026-10-19 while the corrective supervised run was active. The purpose of this round is to test architectural and optimization direction with useful accuracy under the local GPU limit. Release eligibility is a separate decision.

For subsequent development curriculum decisions, use **85% complete-group correctness** for both actor and event binding in each language, replacing the original 90% retention floor. Keep the other retention checks: 95% balanced accuracy on the fixed training probe, 55% canonical development balanced accuracy, and two consecutive passing evaluations. The probe is a subset of training data. Passing retention permits the next curriculum stage; it does not establish held-out reasoning quality.

The original primary supervised run's Chinese actor-group score at step 250 was **89.0625% (57/64 pairs)**, rising to **98.4375%** at step 500. The 90% floor required at least 58/64 at the first check. Both observations pass the amended retention rules, so two consecutive checks are available at step 500. The original frozen 90% policy admitted only one passing check and stopped that run. This was a curriculum-window failure under that policy, not evidence that the model direction was wrong.

The corrective run had already passed the original 90% policy at step 750 and entered joint supervision at step 751 before this amendment. Continue its measured trajectory. Do not rerun merely to pass a lower gate or claim that its joint results represent a step-501 transition: that counterfactual was not trained. Preserve the original protocols, summaries and selections.

The implementation is `DecisionScaling.diagnostic_mastery_rules` in [diagnostic_policy.rb](../../benchmarks/decision/scaling/diagnostic_policy.rb). Newly initialized experiments using `--phase prepare` now freeze the 85% rule before training. Already frozen protocols retain their actual rules, including historical input replays. `--phase audit-diagnostic-policy` reassesses saved histories under both policies without training. Its output records input hashes; it is a retrospective diagnostic, not independent confirmation.

Judge direction from held-out actor/event groups, new combinations, new actors, natural-language performance, and their trends relative to the parent and capacity/data controls. Report useful partial improvements even when a release gate fails. Lowering retention does not repair a near-zero held-out actor-group score, and this experiment cannot establish that larger hardware alone would solve the remaining problems. Existing release criteria are retained for interpreting historical eligibility, rather than used as the sole definition of development progress.
